The First Hour After a Bad Review Decides Whether It's a Crisis or a Non-Event

Consumers now expect a fast response — and a well-handled bad review, addressed quickly, often does more for trust than another five-star one sitting unanswered.

A two-star review lands. It sits there — read by a handful of prospective customers before anyone from the business even notices it exists, let alone responds. By the time a reply finally goes up, days later, it reads less like resolution and more like an afterthought.

19% of consumers now expect a same-day response to a review — more than triple the 6% who expected that just a year earlier — even as overall trust in reviews has fallen to 42%, making how a business actually responds the signal that's replacing blind trust in the star rating itself.

— BrightLocal, Local Consumer Review Survey 2025 ↗

A bad review left unanswered confirms the reader's worst assumption

A prospective customer reading a bad review isn't only judging the incident described — they're judging whether the business ever addressed it. Silence reads as "this is exactly the kind of business that doesn't care," even when the actual issue was a genuine one-off that any business could have.

Why speed matters more than the perfect apology

The value of catching this within the hour isn't crafting flawless PR language — it's that a fast, genuine acknowledgment plus a concrete recovery offer, sent before the review has had time to sit and be read by fifty more prospective customers, frequently gets the reviewer to update or remove it. That almost never happens once a bad review has sat unanswered for a week.
