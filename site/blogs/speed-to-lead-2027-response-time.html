78% of Customers Buy From Whoever Replies First. By 2027, That Gap Only Gets Wider

A landmark study found waiting 30 minutes instead of 5 to contact a lead cuts the odds of qualifying them by 21x. Most businesses' actual response time isn't measured in minutes at all.

A customer rarely sends one inquiry and waits patiently. They send the same question to two or three businesses at once, as standard practice, and whichever replies first is very often the one that gets the sale — regardless of who actually has the better offer.

Waiting 30 minutes instead of 5 to contact a new lead reduces the odds of qualifying that lead by a factor of 21 — and 78% of customers buy from whichever business responds to their inquiry first.

— Lead Response Management study, Oldroyd (MIT Sloan) / InsideSales.com ↗

This gap isn't closing on its own — it's widening

As more businesses automate their first response, the ones still replying manually aren't just average anymore — they're falling further behind a rising bar. "We usually get back within a few hours" used to be a normal answer. Increasingly, it's the reason a customer already bought elsewhere.

What actually closes this gap

Not a bigger sales team — an instant first reply, even a simple one, that keeps the conversation alive until a human can take over. The businesses treating response speed as a real metric, not an afterthought, are the ones positioned to keep winning this by 2027.
