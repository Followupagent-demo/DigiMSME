India's Diagnostic Labs Market Will Nearly Double by 2030 — Home Collection Is Where the Growth Actually Is

A patient wants a home-collection blood test. The booking process asks for too much, too slowly — and they call a competitor instead.

A patient — or more often, a family member booking for someone who can't travel — wants a home blood-collection appointment. The booking form asks for details better suited to a hospital admission than a same-day test, and by the time it's filled out, they've already called somewhere else.

India's diagnostic labs market is projected to grow from $18.41 billion in 2024 to $34.68 billion by 2030 at a 10.90% CAGR, with the home-healthcare services segment alone projected to reach $35.2 billion by 2030.

— TechSci Research ↗

The growth is in home collection — and that's exactly where booking friction costs the most

Patients choosing home collection are actively choosing convenience over a lab visit. A booking flow that adds friction back in — a long form, a phone-only option, a callback that takes hours — defeats the entire reason they picked home collection in the first place, and hands the booking to whichever lab makes it genuinely fast.

What actually wins this booking

WhatsApp booking that takes under a minute, a per-branch Google presence so nearby patients find the closest lab first, and digital reports delivered where the patient already is. As home healthcare heads toward more than doubling by 2030, this is the exact mechanism deciding which labs capture that growth.
