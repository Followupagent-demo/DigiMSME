The GST E-Invoicing Net Is Tightening — What Every MSME Should Know Before FY 2027

The e-invoicing threshold has been lowered repeatedly since 2020. Once a business crosses it, the obligation doesn't go away even if turnover later falls.

E-invoicing started in October 2020 as a rule for very large companies — a ₹500 crore turnover threshold that felt irrelevant to most MSMEs. It's been lowered repeatedly since, and it now applies to a much larger share of small and mid-size businesses than most owners realize.

The GST e-invoicing threshold stands at ₹5 crore aggregate annual turnover for FY 2026-27 — and once a business crosses that threshold in any financial year since 2017-18, the obligation to generate e-invoices for B2B, export, SEZ and deemed-export supplies is permanent, even if turnover later drops below it.

— GST e-invoicing rules, Government of India ↗

This isn't a future rule to plan around. For many MSMEs, it already applies.

A business that crossed ₹5 crore turnover even once in the years since 2017-18 is covered by this mandate right now, regardless of what its turnover looks like today. The compliance risk isn't hypothetical — it's a question of whether the business's own invoicing and billing process is actually generating compliant e-invoices for every relevant transaction, today.

What this actually requires in practice

Invoicing systems that generate compliant e-invoices automatically, not a manual process someone has to remember for every B2B transaction. As the threshold has moved lower every few years, the safer assumption for any growing MSME is that this already applies — and building the habit now avoids a scramble later.
