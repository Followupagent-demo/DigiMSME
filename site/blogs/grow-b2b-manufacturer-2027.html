India's B2B E-Commerce Is Racing Toward $200 Billion by 2030 — Most Manufacturers Are Still Quoting by Email

An RFQ lands on WhatsApp while the shop floor is busy with another customer. By the time it's quoted, the buyer has already bought from whoever answered first.

A genuine buyer inquiry comes in — real specs, real quantity — while the floor is busy with production and stock counts. It sits unread for a day, sometimes more. By the time someone gets to it, the buyer has already ordered from a competitor who replied same-day.

India's B2B online marketplace opportunity is projected to reach $200 billion by 2030, and the Government e-Marketplace (GeM) alone has crossed ₹18.4 lakh crore in cumulative procurement, with micro and small enterprises making up 73% of active sellers.

— Bessemer Venture Partners / Government e-Marketplace ↗

This isn't a small-manufacturer problem — it's most of the market moving digital at once

With micro and small enterprises already making up the majority of active sellers on India's largest public procurement platform, the manufacturers capturing this growth aren't necessarily the biggest ones — they're the ones responding fastest. A same-day quote from a live catalog beats a better-priced quote that arrives four days late, almost every time.

What actually needs to change before 2027

A live catalog buyers can reference in their own RFQ, every inquiry pushed to WhatsApp instead of a shared inbox nobody checks fast enough, and quote templates that make same-day response the default. None of this requires new machinery — just making sure the inquiry that's already arriving gets answered before it goes elsewhere.
