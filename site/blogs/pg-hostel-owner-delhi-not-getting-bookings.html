Own a PG in Delhi? Here's Why Rooms Sit Empty While Enquiries Keep Coming

A student messages five PGs near their college at once. Whichever replies first with real photos and a price usually wins the booking — not the better room.

A parent is helping their child find a PG near a Delhi college or office hub — North Campus, Laxmi Nagar, Mukherjee Nagar, wherever. They message four or five PG owners on WhatsApp within the same ten minutes, describing budget and requirements. Whoever replies first with a clear price, real room photos, and a way to book a visit is the one who gets the visit. The others just never get a callback, because by the time the owner is free, a token amount has already been paid somewhere else.

Why rooms stay empty even when enquiries are strong

Most Delhi PG owners don't have a dedicated front desk — one person is handling move-ins, maintenance complaints, rent collection, AND new enquiries on a personal WhatsApp number, often while physically showing a room to someone else. A missed call during a room tour is a missed booking, not just a missed call — and there's usually no record anyone even asked, so nobody follows up later either.

What actually fixes this

This isn't a bigger marketing-budget problem, it's a response-speed problem — and it has the same fix as every high-enquiry-volume business: a missed call triggers an instant WhatsApp reply with availability, price and photos, instead of silence. Anyone who enquired but didn't book gets a genuine follow-up instead of disappearing into a chat that never reopens.
