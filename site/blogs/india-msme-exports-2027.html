MSMEs Already Drive 48.5% of India's Exports. Getting to $1 Trillion by 2030 Runs Through Them

India's export ambitions aren't an enterprise story — nearly half already comes from businesses the size of the one reading this.

When trade policy discussions talk about India's export ambitions, it's easy to picture large conglomerates. The reality is that almost half of what India actually exports already comes from businesses far smaller than that.

MSMEs account for more than 48.5% of India's exports and around 31.1% of GDP, according to the Ministry of MSME's own Annual Report — and the government is targeting $1 trillion in total merchandise exports by 2030, with $200 billion expected to come through e-commerce.

— Ministry of MSME, Annual Report 2025-26 ↗

The $1 trillion target isn't decided in trade policy meetings — it's decided one RFQ at a time

With MSMEs already responsible for nearly half of exports, hitting a national target this size depends overwhelmingly on exactly these businesses winning more of the inquiries they're already receiving, not a handful of giant new export deals from elsewhere.

What actually moves an individual exporter's share of that number

The same mechanism already closing this gap for exporters and manufacturers specifically: a live catalog, inquiries routed to WhatsApp instead of a shared inbox, and same-day quotes instead of four-day ones. National targets are aggregate outcomes of exactly this kind of individual response speed, repeated across hundreds of thousands of businesses.
