India's B2B Wholesale Trade Is Going Digital by 2030. Distributors Still Taking Orders by Phone Are Running Out of Runway

A retailer calls to restock. Hold music, a hand-read price list, a callback that comes too late — enough friction that they call someone else instead.

A regular retailer wants to restock — genuinely easy business, a repeat order from an existing relationship. And the process of actually placing it involves a hold queue, someone reading out a price list by hand, and enough friction that a retailer in a hurry just calls a different distributor instead.

India's B2B e-commerce market is projected to reach roughly ₹17.3 lakh crore ($200 billion) by 2030, with vertical B2B marketplaces growing at close to a 45% CAGR as general traders shift toward organized digital trade.

— Bessemer Venture Partners ↗

The retailers aren't leaving because of price. They're leaving because reordering is friction.

A distributor doesn't need to win a new retailer's trust from scratch — it already has that with existing accounts. The risk by 2027 is losing them anyway, purely because reordering from a competitor's live catalog takes thirty seconds and reordering by phone takes ten minutes.

What keeps repeat business repeat

A live WhatsApp catalog retailers can browse and order from directly, automatic reorder nudges timed to each retailer's actual cycle, and every order tracked in one place instead of a notebook. The relationship already exists — this just removes the reason to test a competitor.
